Carpet Water Damage in Norton, MA

Carpet dries quickly, but the pad under it holds water. The key question is clean water or contaminated water.

Carpet is easier to dry than most people expect when the water was clean and the response was quick. The pad underneath is the problem. It acts like a sponge and takes days to dry, which is why it is often removed even when the carpet is saved.

If carpet is wet, lift furniture onto blocks or foil, open windows if the weather allows and run a fan. Photograph the area. Do not walk on it more than you must, and call with questions about whether it can be saved.

Clean water versus contaminated water

The category of water decides everything. Clean water from a supply line is Category 1 and carpet can often be dried and kept. Water from a toilet, sewage or flood is Category 3, and carpet and pad should be discarded. See sewage backup cleanup for that case.

Water that sat for a day or two is no longer treated as clean, because bacteria grow in wet carpet. ANSI/IICRC S500 describes the categories and the times, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

How wet carpet is handled

The method changes with what the pad looks like.

What to do in the first hour with wet carpet

Act on the carpet before you worry about the cause. Pull lamps and electronics off the floor, lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic blocks so wood stain does not bleed into the fibers, and remove loose rugs. Open windows if the weather allows and run fans toward the room, but leave the heating on at a normal setting.

Stand on the carpet as little as possible. Walking on saturated pile pushes water into the pad and spreads dirt. If the water came from a toilet, drain or outside, treat it as contaminated, keep children and pets out and skip the do-it-yourself steps.

Why the pad and the tack strips matter

The pad is a foam or fiber layer that can hold many times its weight in water. It dries slowly, it smells when it stays wet, and it is cheap compared with the carpet above it. That is why crews usually replace pad even when the carpet is kept.

Tack strips along the walls are wood, and they absorb water too. A crew checks them and the bottom of the wall, because wet strips can stain carpet edges and hold moisture against the baseboard. Seams and backing are checked for delamination, which happens when the glue layer gives up.

When replacing is the smarter choice

Replacement makes sense when the water was contaminated, when the carpet is old, or when drying would cost close to what new carpet would. A good crew will say so honestly and show you the readings behind the call.

If you keep the carpet, ask for a final moisture reading under it and a clean smell check after a week. A smell that returns means water is still in the pad, the slab or the wall base.
