Flood damage in Norton ranges from a few inches in a basement to water across a main floor. Emergency flood cleanup calls go to our 24/7 line, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available. Floods differ from a pipe break because the water comes from outside and may carry soil, sewage and debris.

Stay out of flood water if you can. It may be electrically live and may contain contaminants. Shut off power at the main only if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and leave the house if water is rising. Call 911 for anyone in danger.

Floods in Norton: what the town's own records say

The Town of Norton's 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness workshop summary describes much of the town as low-lying, with flood-prone roads including Richardson Avenue, Walker Street and Route 123. That is the town's own assessment, and it does not mean every street floods.

To know your own risk, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask the Norton building department what applies to your property. Flood risk can differ block by block, and the map is the official reference. Homes near the water are covered on the Winnecunnet area page.

Why flood water is treated as contaminated

Standards used by the industry sort water into three categories. Clean supply water is Category 1. Water with significant contamination is Category 2. Water that is grossly contaminated, including surface flood water and sewage, is Category 3. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes these categories, and homeowners can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

Flood water usually counts as Category 3 because it crosses yards and roads before it enters the house. That changes the work. Porous materials such as carpet, pad and insulation that were soaked are generally removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized before drying finishes.

After the water goes down

Do not turn the heating system or appliances on until a professional has checked them. Boilers, furnaces and electrical panels that took water need inspection before use. Open windows for ventilation if conditions allow and wear gloves and boots while you work.

Keep a list of discarded items with photos, and ask your insurer or the town what rules apply to disposal of flood debris. Check with the Norton building department about any permits needed for repairs.

What to do first, and what not to do

Flood water can carry electricity and contamination, so protect yourself first.

Safety steps

  • Stay out of flood water, which may be live with electricity or contaminated
  • Do not turn on the furnace, boiler or appliances that took water
  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash well afterward
  • Do not eat food that touched flood water
