Water damage restoration in Easton, MA is handled from the same 24/7 emergency line that answers in Norton. Easton borders Norton to the northeast, and its villages are covered by separate pages: South Easton and North Easton.

ZIP 02334 is a post office box code, so most street addresses in town use 02356, 02357 or 02375. This page covers the town as a whole and the villages are covered in more detail on their own pages.

Emergency help for Easton homeowners

An emergency in Easton is any water that is still arriving, any water near electrical equipment or any water from a drain. The 24/7 line takes those calls day and night, and the dispatcher will ask where the water is entering and how deep it is.

If you are on a street that floods during storms, tell the dispatcher so the crew arrives with the right pumps. Easton's hazard reports list basement flooding as a townwide problem during storms.

Easton's low spots, culverts and swamp

Easton's municipal planning report on Mass.gov names several trouble spots. Bay Road near number 224 is described as the confluence of three culverts. Sawmill Pond Road at Bay Road has localized flooding tied to the Sawmill Pond dam. On Prospect Road, a broken culvert is noted, and a catch basin near Prospect Street and the railway floods regularly. The culvert on Purchase Street near the dog leg at the Easton Country Club needs a size and condition check.

The report also notes that the Hockomock Swamp provides flood control, and that mounded septic tanks can saturate ground downstream. Saturated ground in many parts of town during heavy rain is a recurring theme, which is why basement pumping is a frequent job. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the Easton building department what applies.

If Water Is Still Spreading in Easton: Four Quick Checks

These checks protect people first and property second.

Electrical

Do not step in water near outlets or the panel. Wait for the utility or an electrician.

Source

Find and stop the source. If it is rainwater, note where it enters.

Contents

Photograph the water line and the damage before cleanup begins.

What a 24/7 line means for Easton

The emergency line is staffed every hour of every day, so an Easton call at three in the morning is answered like a call at noon. That matters because water damage grows with time. Drywall, insulation and carpet pad absorb water and the damage compounds.

The dispatcher confirms your address and village, since Easton's villages have different ZIP codes, and sends a crew from Norton. You do not need to know which ZIP you are in to get help.

Dams, ponds and Easton basements

Easton's planning report lists dams at French Pond, Langwater Pond, Long (Ames) Pond, Old Cabot Pond, New Pond, Shovel Shop Pond and Morse Pond, and says dams have been breached at Flyaway Pond and Monte Pond. Ponds and brooks throughout town raise the groundwater in wet seasons.

A house near a pond or brook is more likely to have a damp basement after heavy rain. Gutters that discharge near the foundation, a working sump and a battery backup for it are practical protections. Ask a plumber or drainage contractor what suits your lot.

What to do first, and what not to do

Easton basements often take water through floors and walls, so start with safety.

Safety steps

  • Stay out of water near outlets, cords or the panel
  • Close the main valve if the source is inside
  • Move valuables to dry, high ground
  • Do not drive through flooded roads
