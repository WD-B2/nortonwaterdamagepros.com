Storms push water through roofs, windows and foundations. Cleanup starts with protection from more rain and ends with a dry structure.

Storm water damage in Norton arrives when rain finds a way in, whether through a lifted shingle, a window, a flooded window well or a sump pump that cannot keep up. Emergency storm cleanup calls go to our 24/7 line, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available during busy weather.

If it is safe, move belongings away from the wet area, catch drips with buckets and shut off power to any room where water reaches outlets. Photograph the damage and any broken openings. Never go up on a wet roof, and wait for conditions to ease before anyone checks the exterior.

When a storm becomes an emergency

A storm turns into an emergency when water is inside and still entering. A roof opening during heavy rain, a basement taking water through the wall or a window blown in all need quick containment, because the next band of rain makes the loss larger.

Norton's own planning shows why basements and roads matter. The town's 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness workshop identified much of Norton as low-lying and listed flooded roads at Richardson Avenue, Walker Street and Route 123. For storm warnings and watches, the National Weather Service issues the official forecasts, and they are a better guide than any single news report.

Storm response in four moves

The order matters. Protecting the opening comes before drying, or the work is wasted.

1

Contain

Tarps or boards over a roof or window opening, buckets for drips, contents moved.

2

Extract

Water is pumped and extracted. See water extraction for the method.

3

Dry

Moisture is mapped and structural drying equipment is set. See structural drying.

4

Document

Meter readings and photos are kept for the insurance file.

Temporary protection is not a roof repair. A roofer finishes that work once the weather allows.

After the storm: a safety checklist

Safety comes before cleanup. These steps protect you and your claim.

Before you start any storm cleanup

  • Stay off roofs, ladders and downed power lines
  • Turn off power to any wet room if the panel is dry and reachable
  • Photograph every wet room and opening before moving anything
  • Do not use a regular vacuum on standing water
  • Call your insurer and the restoration line as soon as it is safe

Wind and rain damage are often covered under homeowners policies while flood damage is usually excluded. Your insurer can explain how your policy treats each.

Documenting storm damage for a claim

Take wide photos of each room and close photos of each stain, then photos of the exterior if it is safe. Save weather alerts, the date and time of the storm and any emergency repair receipts.

Ask your insurer about your wind, hail and water deductibles before the crew starts. Some policies set a separate percentage deductible for storm events, and knowing the number helps you decide the scope.

What affects the cost of storm cleanup

Storm jobs vary more than most.

Entry points

One roof breach is simpler than several.

Water volume

Standing water takes pumps and extraction, rain through a ceiling takes drying.

Contamination

Water that came from outside may be Category 2 or 3.

Demand

Many homes are hit at once, so scheduling can stretch.

Temporary vs permanent fix

Tarping is quick, roof replacement is separate.

Check your policy for wind, rain and flood language. Ask whether your deductible for storm damage is different from other claims.
