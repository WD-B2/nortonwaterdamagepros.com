Winnecunnet is the part of Norton around Lake Winnecunnet, a kettle pond east of the town center and north of Interstate 495. Homes near a pond share a few concerns, among them groundwater, storm runoff and power outages that stop a sump pump.

If water is in your home, call and describe it. The dispatcher asks about depth, source and electrical hazards, and explains what to do before a crew arrives.

Where Winnecunnet is

Wikipedia describes Lake Winnecunnet as the town's only natural body of water, a kettle pond formed more than 13,000 years ago and located near King Philip's Cave. Roads near the water include King Philip Road, Bay Road and Winnecunnet Drive. We place the area by the lake and those roads because no official boundary is published.

If your street runs toward the lake, you are in the area this page covers. The nearest villages on our pages are Norton Center and Chartley.

What the town report says about the area

Norton's Community Resilience Building report, posted on Mass.gov, names the Winnecunnet Pond area and the stormwater systems around Winnecunnet and Chartley as vulnerable. It also lists Bay Road and the Winnecunnet Pond homes among the places noted for power outage or flood vulnerability.

Those are planning findings for the area. They do not say that any particular home has flooded, and we do not claim one has. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, ask the Norton building department about local rules, and ask your insurer about sump pump and flood coverage.

If Water Is Still Spreading in Winnecunnet: Prepare Before the Crew Arrives

These steps take minutes and need no tools. They protect people first and the claim record second.

Stop the source if it is safe

Close the main or fixture valve only if you can reach it without standing in water near electrical equipment.

Photograph first

Take wide and close photos of every wet room before moving anything.

Lift what you can

Move papers, electronics and small furniture to dry, higher spots.

Give the crew a path

Clear a route from the door to the wet area and note where a truck can park.

If the water came from outside or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep people and pets out.

Insurance and flood coverage

Photograph the water line and the damage before you move anything, and note the date and time you found it. A restoration crew can add readings and a written scope of work.

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and often needs separate flood insurance. Sudden and accidental water from inside the house is often treated differently. Ask your insurer, because coverage depends on cause and policy terms.

Extraction, drying and contaminated water

Standing water is pumped out first. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure, and readings are repeated until materials reach a dry standard under ANSI/IICRC S500. Outside water is treated as contaminated, so carpet, pad and some drywall are usually removed rather than saved.

Do not rely on household fans or a vacuum for it. See flood damage cleanup and sewage backup cleanup.
