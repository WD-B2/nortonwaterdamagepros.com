A water loss at a business is an emergency because closed doors cost money. Emergency commercial water damage calls in Norton go to our 24/7 line, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available. A building manager or owner who calls early can limit the damage and the downtime.

Shut off the water supply, turn off power to affected areas, protect inventory and records and photograph everything. Tell your landlord or property manager if you lease, and notify your insurer.

What makes commercial work different

Commercial spaces have larger floor areas, shared walls, ceilings with sprinkler lines and tenants who need to reopen. A leak in a suite can affect neighbors above and below, and the lease may spell out who pays for what.

Routes through the building matter too. Crews work around business hours and protect stock, records and electronics. Priorities are set with the owner or manager before work starts. Buildings around the Town Common are covered on the Norton Center page.

The first call from a building owner or manager

Tell the dispatcher the type of business, the floor area that is wet, whether the water is still running and whether there are tenants or neighbors affected. That lets a crew arrive with the right pumps and enough drying equipment for the space, instead of making a second trip.

Have someone who can approve access and sign for the work on site or by phone. If you lease, find out whether the landlord's insurer or yours handles the building portion, because that decides who gets the invoice.

Protecting stock, records and equipment

Water loss at a business is often about contents. Move inventory off the floor onto racks or pallets if you can do so safely, and unplug electronics only if power is off and the area is dry. Paper records can sometimes be frozen to stop damage until they can be dried, so ask before throwing any away.

Keep a list with photos of what was damaged. Insurers ask for an inventory, and the list is easier to build on day one than after the area has been cleaned up.

Reopening and working around operations

A commercial crew can often split a space into zones, so a dry area reopens while a wet area stays closed. Containment barriers and dehumidifiers keep the damp zone from affecting the rest of the space.

Health-regulated businesses such as restaurants should check with the Norton Board of Health before reopening after a sewage or contamination event. Ask the board what it needs to see, and keep the cleanup documentation ready.

What to do first, and what not to do

Staff and customers come before stock and records.

Safety steps

  • Shut off water and power to the affected area
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet or sagging areas
  • Do not move electronics or records unless the area is dry and safe
  • Do not reopen until the crew confirms the space is safe
