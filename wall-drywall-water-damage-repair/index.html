Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Norton, MA

Drywall wicks water upward and loses strength when wet. A crew finds the wet line, dries or removes it and repairs the wall.

Wet drywall softens, stains and can grow mold behind the paint. Water moves up the wall from the floor like a candle wick, so the wet line is often higher than it looks. Quick drying saves some drywall. If it has been wet for days or is crumbling, it is removed.

Photograph the stain and the area around it. Turn off the water source if you know it. Do not paint or caulk over a stain. Call and a crew can measure moisture to see how far it goes.

How far does water climb a wall

Paper-faced drywall draws water upward by capillary action. A one-inch puddle can wet drywall several inches above the floor. Insulation inside the wall holds water and keeps wetting the back of the board.

Staining, bubbling paint, soft spots, bowing and a musty smell all signal moisture. A meter finds the boundary between wet and dry, and a flood cut at that line removes the wet section.

Signs the wall is wet before the paint shows it

Drywall can be wet while the paint still looks fine. A cool spot to the touch, a faint musty smell near the baseboard, nails or screws that start to show through the paint, and baseboards that pull away from the wall are early signs. A hand-held meter reads the board without cutting it.

Thermal cameras show temperature differences, and a wet wall evaporates, so it reads cooler. A camera is a screening tool, not proof, which is why meters confirm each suspicious spot.

Plaster, textured walls and older homes

Many Norton-area homes have older plaster or plaster-and-lath walls in places. Plaster handles brief wetting better than paper-faced board, but it can detach from the lath and it dries slowly. Textured ceilings and walls need extra care to match after a patch.

If your walls are not standard drywall, tell the crew when you call. The repair plan changes, and so does the time it takes to match the finish. Painted-over wallpaper and old lead-containing paint also change how walls are cut and cleaned, so mention the age of the home.

After the repair: painting and prevention

Once the framing and board read dry, new drywall is taped, finished and primed. Paint a full wall corner to corner where you can, because a patch of new paint on an old wall shows in side light. Use a stain-blocking primer over any remaining mark from the original leak.

Fix the cause before the final coat. A small leak that is left in place will wet the new board too. Ask the plumber or roofer to confirm the source is repaired, and keep their note with your records.
