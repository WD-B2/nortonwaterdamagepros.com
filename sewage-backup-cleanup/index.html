Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Norton, MA

Sewage water is Category 3 and unsafe to handle. Cleanup removes it and the materials it soaked, then cleans and dries the area.

Sewage backup is an emergency because the water is contaminated from the moment it enters the house. Emergency sewage cleanup calls in Norton go to our 24/7 line, and a crew in protective gear can be dispatched the same day when available.

Keep everyone out of the affected area, especially children and pets. Do not flush or run water, since that sends more into the backup. Do not try to clean it with towels. Wash your hands if you touched anything, and ask your doctor if you have health questions.

Symptoms people search for before they know it is sewage

Common signs are a gurgling toilet, a floor drain that bubbles, water backing into a tub or shower when you run the washer, a sewer smell in the basement and dark water coming up from a drain.

These symptoms point to a blockage somewhere in the line, either inside the house or in the lateral that connects to the main. A plumber clears the blockage. The restoration crew handles what came up.

Norton: sewer or septic?

Not every Norton home connects to a public sewer. Some use private septic systems. Ask the Norton building department or the Board of Health which applies to your address. The cleanup steps are similar, but the cause and the repair differ, and a septic overflow may involve a septic contractor instead of the town.

Backup coverage is also separate. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes that sewer and drain backup is typically covered only by an endorsement to a homeowners policy, so check yours.

Sewage emergency: how cleanup works

The goal is to remove the contamination, not just the water.

1

Contain and protect

Crew suits up, the area is sealed off and the source is identified.

2

Remove water and solids

Pumps and extraction units remove liquid, and solids are bagged.

3

Remove porous materials

Carpet, pad, insulation and soaked drywall are discarded.

4

Clean, sanitize, dry

Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then the structure is dried. See structural drying.

Cleaning follows the Category 3 guidance in ANSI/IICRC S500. Ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

What to do while you wait for the crew

Keep the area closed off and shut the door if there is one. Turn off the heating or cooling system if the ductwork runs through the affected space, so air does not carry odors into other rooms. Do not use fans on the affected area, which can spread contamination.

Take photos from the doorway and write down what the water touched. Gather medications, documents and valuables from clean areas only, and leave wet items where they are for the crew to sort.

Deodorizing and the final check

Odor can linger in porous materials and in cracks. After removal and cleaning, crews use air scrubbers and deodorizers, and they check that no wet or stained materials remain. A smell that persists after cleaning usually means something was missed or the drain is still leaking.

Ask for a walkthrough before the rebuild begins, and ask how the crew handled the Category 3 materials and disposal.
